## A. Exact metric-balancing reduction for the abstract slope theorem
Use the conventions of ARAK.1120: K totally complex, t infinite places, source integral maps B_i into C, fixed lattice N_i, given height bounds h_i. For nonzero B_i define a_iw=log||B_i||_w^2, actual mean a_i=(sum_w a_iw)/t<=h_i. Change the squared source norm at place w by factor exp(a_iw+h_i-a_i). The new local squared operator norm is exp(a_i-h_i)<=1, and the mean logarithm of the source metric factor is exactly h_i. For B_i=0 choose constant source squared norm factor exp(h_i); it stays zero and the same slope identity holds. For every nonzero K-subspace of dimension r the archimedean real covolume is multiplied by exp(r sum_w(a_iw+h_i-a_i)), because each complex place contributes real dimension 2r. Hence in the paper's squared-norm slope convention, the slope of EVERY nonzero submodule and quotient drops by h_i (rank over Z=2tr), not just the full lattice. The HN filtration is unchanged and all slopes translate. No finite lattice, integral image or source prime is changed. Uniform metric scaling plus zero-mean balancing suffices; no number-field unit is needed.

Critical source-to-argument check: in the abstract theorem the X_i(w) arithmetic frames and the Y_i analytic frames are independent, with only first-r-row equality involving Y. Changing X_i therefore does not require changing Y_i or the moment data. All original slope assumptions can be rewritten with height bounds zero and translated slopes, and s,U,S unchanged. This is a valid algebraic reduction of the stated abstract theorem, NOT a change to the original geometry or LinkDIV data. The previous fixed-metric height counterexample is not invalidated: it applies when X is fixed. For grouping choose direct sum of the translated source metrics and grouped Y_G=direct sum sqrt(gamma_i)Y_i, grouped tilde map = concatenation of sqrt(gamma_i)tilde B_i, gamma_G=1. First rows match the original requirement exactly and Q unchanged. Integrality on the direct sum holds. Within-group moment error still increases by the previous checkpoint's cross terms; no new bound on geometry.

Direct-sum mean slope is rank-weighted; maximal slope is the maximum of the component maxima. Proof of latter: for any submodule A in E⊕F project to F, kernel A∩E. Quotient norm dominates the norm induced by F (projection is a contraction); covolume multiplicativity for saturated kernel in A gives deg(A)<=deg(kernel)+deg(image). Apply each maximum slope bound, and the converse follows by embedding individual submodules. Induction gives arbitrary finite sums. The discriminant constant is harmless by rank additivity. Full direct-sum HN slopes likewise merge sorted component lists using semistability of equal-slope orthogonal direct sums and the direct-sum filtrations. This does NOT mean that slopes of mixed submodules are unchanged by independent zero-mean twists: e.g. opposite twists at two places on two equal lines increase the norm of the diagonal line, although its component line slopes stay fixed.

## B. Optimal adaptive scalar grouping at a place (new self-contained reduction)
For each nonzero contraction A_i with ||A_i||=1 (or a norm-one PSD M_i=A_iA_i*), fix p_i=d_i/D_G>0, sum p_i=1. Instead of enforcing sum t_i<=1, optimize over actual local arithmetic overlap:

    F* = max_{t_i>0, sum_i t_i M_i <= I} sum_i p_i log t_i.
    H(p) = -sum_i p_i log p_i.
    F* = -H(p) - max_{Z PSD, tr Z=1} sum_i p_i log tr(Z M_i).

Proof: For any density Z let a_i=tr(ZM_i)>0. Feasibility implies sum_i t_i a_i<=1, hence by Jensen sum p_i log(t_i a_i/p_i)<=log(sum t_i a_i)<=0. This gives upper bound sum p_i log(p_i/a_i). Maximize concave G(Z)=sum p_i log a_i over compact densities; finite point I/D exists, boundary where any a_i=0 has value -infinity, so a maximizer Z* exists with all a_i>0. For any density W the directional derivative at Z* toward W is nonpositive: sum p_i tr(W M_i)/a_i <= sum p_i=1. Test W=vv* for every unit vector; S=sum p_i M_i/a_i<=I. Thus t_i=p_i/a_i is feasible and saturates Jensen, proving equality. No interchange of noncompact extrema or unproved strong duality required. For nonzero contractions with norm<1 same identity holds but t_i can exceed 1 and the cost can be negative; use norm-one hypothesis when asserting 0<=cost<=H. Zero maps excluded from formula (must separate), since a_i=0 identically and t unbounded. This is an optimization of source metric choices, not a change to finite lattice or period-map identities.

Cost -F* is in [0,H(p)] for norm-one M_i. If all share a common unit top-eigenvector then cost H(p). If images lie in mutually orthogonal classes with common identical norm-one operator per class, exact solution t_i=p_i/P_class where P_class=sum weights in class; loss = H(p)-H(P_class). If weighted rank-one tight frame sum p_i M_i=I/D, take t_i=D p_i and density I/D, equality gives cost H(p)-log D. Two equal-weight rank-one projections with overlap c=|<u,v>| have t=1/(1+c), optimal cost log(1+c), exactly certified by projector onto phase-aligned u+v. This interpolates from orthogonal to identical and is strictly better than log 2 for c<1. These are exact results in the abstract local optimization model, no novelty claim in convex analysis literature.

Numerical algorithm with explicit primal-dual gap: for arbitrary density Z, a_i>0, set t_i=p_i/a_i, S=sum t_i M_i, lambda=max(1,||S||). Feasible primal t_i/lambda, so F* lies between sum p_i log t_i-log lambda and sum p_i log t_i. Gap log lambda; maximize G(Z) by moving toward a top eigenvector density of S, with line search. If exact certification needed, bound lambda with rational PSD certificate instead of relying on float eigenvalues. Across all infinite places choose t_i(w) independently; mean source slope decreases by the weighted average of the local costs. To use in actual slope theorem one must also bound maximal individual slope shifts and keep normalized moments with Y independent, as in A. No reason actual arithmetic operator overlaps resemble the ideal analytic MUB model.

## C. Fixed metric variant using units, and a distinct uniformity obstruction
If X frames cannot be changed, a possible alternative is scaling each B_i by an algebraic unit u_i. Integral images B_i(N_i) are unchanged as modules since u_i N_i=N_i; actual height unchanged by product formula. Local squared-log profile shifts by vector (log|sigma_w(u_i)|^2)_w of zero mean. Dirichlet unit theorem makes these vectors a full lattice in the mean-zero hyperplane (totally complex). With an infinity-norm covering radius rho_K, choose a unit making each local log norm <= actual h(B_i)+rho_K. Then horizontal group height <= max_i h(B_i)+rho_K+log g. A crude finite rho_K bound is half the sum of infinity norms of any independent full-rank unit log vectors, by rounding real coefficients. This is only a fixed-field guarantee. Uniformity as K varies is not automatic: for K=F(i), F real quadratic, each squared-log vector of u arises from totally positive unit Norm_{K/F}u in F, so in two-place mean-zero coordinates every nonzero step is at least log epsilon_F, where epsilon_F>1 generates F units modulo torsion (or a larger step). Thus rho_K>= (log epsilon_F)/2. Fundamental units of distinct real quadratic fields have unbounded sizes: if bounded, their degree-two integer minimal polynomials would have bounded coefficients (conjugate has modulus reciprocal), so only finitely many fields occur. Consequently no uniform rho bound depending only on degree four. This does not preclude controlled cyclotomic subfamilies or a sufficiently large allowed height error; need actual field estimates. Unit scaling may also alter required analytic pairings and fixed algebraic identities; within abstract slope theorem Y_i can be replaced by Y_i/sigma_v(u_i) to preserve first rows, but original arithmetic family requires extra compatibility. Do not silently apply it there.
