## A. Bridge from actual localization kernel to dual module
Let K be totally complex and contain mu_p if p odd, mu_4 if p=2; U=G_{K(mu_{p^infty})}, C=G_K/U, an open subgroup of the principal cyclotomic units, hence Z_p. Let A=Q_p/Z_p(-r), r>=1. Define D_p as in GC.1298: continuous homomorphisms U->Q_p/Z_p vanishing on U intersect all finite inertia groups and on U intersect the decomposition groups above p. Put X=D_p^vee. For any finite cyclotomic layer K_h/K of degree p^h, U and D_p do not change. Let gamma generate C, u=chi_p(gamma), and Gamma act on X by contragredient conjugation action.

Claim: the Pontryagin dual of S_{p,r}(K_h) is isomorphic to coker(((u^r Gamma)^(p^h))-1) on X. This is a standard descent argument independently reconstructed from the actual catalogue definitions, not a new literature theorem.

Proof: the action of U on A is trivial. H1(C_h,A)=0 because every continuous 1-cocycle is determined by its value at a generator, and multiplication by u^(-r p^h)-1 is surjective on the divisible group A. Nonzero scalar follows from r>0 and infinite order u. H2(C_h,A)=0 by cd_p(Z_p)=1. Inflation-restriction for absolute Galois groups therefore identifies H1(G_{K_h},A) with invariant homomorphisms U->A. At a finite place above p the cyclotomic image of its local decomposition group is open, infinite procyclic (finite extension of Q_p); the same H1 argument shows restriction to U intersect that local group is injective. Thus vanishing there is equivalent to local class zero. At places not above p, inertia maps trivially into the cyclotomic quotient, and A is unramified; vanishing on inertia descends through the closed normal subgroup generated by those inertia groups. At p local vanishing also forces inertia zero after restriction to U. Totally complex avoids real-place corrections. These two directions show S_{p,r}(K_h) is exactly the twisted invariants in D_p, rather than merely a subspace. With (g f)(v)=f(g^-1 v g), the twisted action is u^(-r) times the untwisted action on D_p; its contragredient action on X is u^r Gamma. Exact Pontryagin duality changes invariants into coinvariants, yielding the claimed quotient. Compact X finitely generated over Z_p ensures the image is closed.

For the source's E possibly not containing i, use E'=E(i), degree at most 2. Restriction S_{2,r}(E)->S_{2,r}(E') has kernel inside H1(Gal(E'/E),A^{G_{E'}}). The coefficient group is cyclic finite 2-primary (the cyclotomic image of E' contains a nontrivial principal generator). For C2 acting on such a group B, H1=ker(1+g)/(g-1)B is cyclic and killed by 2; length <=1. Thus length S(E)<=1+length S(E') whenever the latter is finite. Cannot blindly assume exact descent over E without adjoining i or handling the finite torsion in the cyclotomic image. If E'=E the correction is 0.

## B. Uniform cyclotomic base exponent for the varying fields
Set L=F(i,sqrt(-q),sqrt(-d)) with F inside Q(mu_m). Both q,d are odd primes congruent to 3 modulo 4, hence the imaginary quadratic discriminants are -q,-d; the fields embed into Q(mu_q),Q(mu_d). By the CRT L is contained in Q(mu_lcm(m,4,q,d)); write c=max(2,v_2(m)). The subgroup 1+2^c Z_2 acts trivially on L intersect Q(mu_{2^infty}), since it can be lifted with identity on the odd conductor factors. Also chi_2(G_L) is contained in 1+4 Z_2 because L contains i. Consequently chi_2(G_L)=1+2^a Z_2 for some 2<=a<=c, independent upper bound in q,d. A generator u satisfies v_2(u-1)=a. Now E(i)=L Q(mu_{2^{nu+2}}) because Q(mu_{2^{nu+2}})^+ together with i generates the full cyclotomic field. Its cyclotomic image is exactly 1+2^{max(a,nu+2)} Z_2. Thus the layer parameter is h=max(0,nu+2-a). This makes the earlier scalar valuation replacement uniform in q,d, without assuming that a=2. If the conductor contains a large power of 2, a may be larger; treating a=2 uniformly was a potential error.

## C. Exact image growth including singular free lifts and torsion
Let X be generated by n elements, Gamma a continuous pro-p action as in previous checkpoint. Choose free lift B of the twisted action u^r Gamma, so B mod p unipotent; choose s=ceil(log_p n)+1 for p=2, ceil(log_p n) for odd p. Then B^(p^s)=I+p^b C with b=2 or 1 respectively. For any near-identity free matrix P=I+p^b C, geometric sum sum_{j=0}^{p-1}P^j=pQ where Q is integral and Q=I mod p. Indeed the linear term coefficient p(p-1)/2 times p^b divided by p is divisible by p for odd p, and for p=2 b>=2 ensures it; higher terms have at least the same divisibility. Thus (P^p-I)Z_p^n=p(P-I)Z_p^n since Q invertible. Iterating yields (P^(p^k)-I)Z_p^n=p^k(P-I)Z_p^n. Projecting through the original free presentation gives EXACT image equality on X even when torsion is present and det(P-I)=0, with no need to divide by p on X itself. Let I=(P-I)X, where P here denotes the descended automorphism. If X/I has finite length, then len X/p^k I = len X/I + len I/p^k I <= len X/I + n k, since submodules of an n-generated module over a DVR are generated by <=n elements. For h<s use monotonicity of the image inclusions. This handles exceptional r via an initial finite length instead of a potentially singular free determinant. Counterexample to dropping p=2 depth: P=-1 on Z_2, P^2-I=0 whereas 2(P-I)Z_2=4Z_2 nonzero. Counterexample to a uniform bound on initial exceptional length: X=Z/p^a with twisted action identity; length a arbitrary. Neither counterexample contradicts the image-growth theorem.

## D. Local prefix sharpening for a fixed exceptional set
Reuse the prior nonsplit lift and maxima r_i over 1..T. For each r !=r_i the valuation w_i(r)<=a+s+v_p(r-r_i). For any prefix M<=T, among r<=M other than r_i, valuations of differences are <=K=floor(log_p(T-1)); number divisible by p^j <=M/p^j+1 for each 1<=j<=K (works even if r_i outside prefix). Therefore sum_{r<=M,r!=r_i}v_p(r-r_i)<= M/(p-1)+K. With E_T the union of at most n maxima, H=max(h,s), the nonexceptional aggregate satisfies

sum_{r<=M,r notin E_T} len coker f_h(r) <= n[(a+H)M + M/(p-1)+K].

This is better than using T in the aggregate when M is much smaller than T, and removes the multiplicative log T term from a naive pointwise bound. It keeps the same fixed E_T for the whole window. On the source's x interval, take T=floor(X^401), M=t(x)-1<=x^401; X<=x^J so K=O(log x), h=O(log x). For p=2, with n and a uniform in q,d, the actual nonexceptional localization loss at E is O(x^401 log x) including the at-most-one correction per twist. No appeal to GRH. This is negligible compared with exp(x^(4epsilon/5)) for fixed positive epsilon as x grows.

## E. Exceptional small twists: conditional reconstruction of the two-adic branch
The catalogue's GC.0086 assumes the uniform integral H2 bound: log |H2(K,Z_p(r+1))| <= exp((2r[K:Q])^C_K log(2|disc K|)) for the specified roots of unity. Apply at the fixed layer L_s (not at E, whose degree grows). From finite Poitou–Tate, finite Sha^1 with coefficient Z/p^k(-r) has cardinality bounded by H2 with Z/p^k(r+1). Higher finite vanishing implies the maps between these H2 groups are surjective; finiteness of the finite cohomology groups gives Mittag-Leffler, hence continuous integral H2 projects onto each finite H2. Every class in the discrete localization kernel is represented at some finite coefficient stage where all local restrictions vanish (finite number of places and commuting H1 with filtered direct limits). Thus its order/length bounded by integral H2; this uses the standard finite-to-compact limit comparison, not a raw Lean source audit. Combined with A and C, lengths at arbitrary h are bounded by len S(L_s)+n max(0,h-s), even at exact resonance.

Since L_s is contained in a cyclotomic field of conductor dividing fixed constant times qd, its degree is uniformly bounded by 8[F:Q]*2^s (adjoining i and two square roots contributes a factor at most 8). Its log discriminant is O(1+log(qd)) via conductor-discriminant for abelian extensions, or the discriminant tower formula and the fixed quadratic/cyclotomic generators. Source assumptions log(qd)<=X^(epsilon/(4J))<=x^(epsilon/4). For r<=x^eta with eta C_K<=epsilon/4, the exponent inside exp is O(x^(epsilon/2)), so the contribution of at most n such r is exp(O(x^(epsilon/2)))+O(log x). This is asymptotically strictly below exp(x^(4epsilon/5)) even after adding the polynomial nonexceptional contribution. If each exceptional exponent is either <=x^eta or >t(x) for the chosen entire window, the two-adic bound in GC.0086 follows from these hypotheses. The source already uses this window strategy; no novelty claim. The window existence itself was studied earlier; this pass does not independently reprove it or the uniform generator theorem or uniform H2 theorem. Odd-prime finite-base and character-eigenspace recombination are still separate requirements. A direct application of integral H2 at growing layer E would give an exponent involving a growing degree and fail the desired small bound.
