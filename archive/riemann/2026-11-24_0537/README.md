## A. Exact normalized moment regrouping theorem
Let C_i:H_i -> H (finite dimensional Hilbert spaces), d_i=dim H_i>0, R=sum_i d_i, D_*>0. Set K_ij=C_i* C_j and Q=sum_i C_i C_i*. Suppose sum_i ||K_ii-I||_HS^2 <= e R and ||K_ij||_HS^2 <= B d_i d_j/D_* for distinct i,j, with B>=0. Partition I into nonempty groups G, take H_G=orthogonal direct sum H_i, C_G((v_i))=sum_i C_i v_i, D_G=sum_{i in G} d_i. Then exactly:

    sum_G C_G C_G* = Q,
    ||C_G* C_G-I||_HS^2 = sum_{i in G} ||K_ii-I||_HS^2 + sum_{i,j in G,i!=j} ||K_ij||_HS^2,
    ||C_G* C_J||_HS^2 = sum_{i in G,j in J} ||K_ij||_HS^2  (G!=J).

Proof: in orthogonal direct sum bases each block of the Gram is K_ij, and squared HS norm is the sum of squared block norms. All ordered off-diagonal pairs are counted. Thus the off-diagonal bound B is preserved exactly, the normalized full-target moment eta is unchanged, and aggregate diagonal defect satisfies

    e_group <= e + B/(R D_*) [sum_G D_G^2 - sum_i d_i^2] <= e + B max_G D_G/D_*.

This proof works for noncommuting complex maps and arbitrary group sizes. The identity requires already-normalized C_i; if original period maps have differing normalizations, concatenate the normalized maps, not the unweighted originals and a guessed scalar. It is an abstract Hilbert-space result.

Corollary: if original d_i<=3D_*/Gamma^2, e=O(Gamma^-1), B bounded, partition into groups with at most floor(Gamma) indices, for Gamma>=2. Then max D_G/D_*<=3/Gamma, e_group=O(Gamma^-1). Number of group labels is ceil(|I|/floor(Gamma)), approximately a factor Gamma fewer, with identical full Q. This does not decrease the number of underlying original arithmetic forms or operations needed to construct them. More aggressive grouping with group cardinality floor(Gamma^2 x^-2h) (when >=1) gives max D_G/D_*<=3x^-2h and diagonal defect O(Gamma^-1+x^-2h). If |I| has order Gamma^2 x^C_H, the number of abstract group labels is polynomial in x, of order x^(C_H+2h), but AGR.0100 only states a lower bound on |I|, so do not assume a matching upper bound. The original normalized proviso does not hold for these groups, but this corollary is derived from valid original moments, not by circular reapplication of LinkS_at. The group dimension cannot be increased indefinitely with small diagonal defect: rank(C_G*C_G)<=D, and if its trace equals D_G then ||C_G*C_G-I||_HS^2 /D_G >= D_G/D-1 (rank/trace Cauchy-Schwarz). No RH consequence on its own.

## B. Explicit ideal-model demonstration and partition optimization
For odd prime p, write zeta=exp(2pi i/p), v_(a,b)(t)=p^-1/2 zeta^(a t^2+b t), for a,b,t in F_p. For fixed a the p vectors form an orthonormal basis by the finite geometric sum. For a!=a', |sum_t zeta^(A t^2+B t)|^2=p with A=a-a' nonzero: write t=s+h in the double sum, the exponent is 2Ahs+Ah^2+Bh; summing s kills h!=0 and h=0 contributes p. Computational basis is mutually unbiased to all quadratic bases. Thus p+1 orthonormal bases form a tight frame, Q=(p+1)I, normalized eta=0.

Use individual vectors as original one-dimensional C_i, D_*=D=p and Gamma=sqrt(p); dimension proviso d_i Gamma^2/D_*=1, R=p(p+1), original off-diagonal bound B=1, e=0. Group each full basis together: p+1 blocks of dimension p, zero diagonal defect, cross HS^2=p=D_G D_J/D, identical Q. The label count falls by factor p=Gamma^2, even though worst-case group bound A would permit O(1) error. This shows normalized moment axioms by themselves do NOT impose Gamma^-2 dimension scaling or an exponential number of distinct labels. It is the standard MUB example transferred to the normalization problem, not a model of automorphic forms or their arithmetic data; Gamma's relation to x,m,f here is artificial, and no uniform number-field realization is claimed.

For any disjoint sets A,B of frame vectors (basis labels may differ), p||C_A*C_B||_HS^2 equals the number of ordered cross-pairs from DIFFERENT bases. For a group A, p||C_A*C_A-I||_HS^2 = |A|^2-sum_a n_a(A)^2, where n_a is its basis occupancy. Thus grouping within orthogonal bases is optimal; grouping r entire bases together instead gives diagonal defect per dimension r-1, proving arbitrary merging is not harmless.

General equal-size partition diagnostic: for N original labels with g dividing N, randomly permute and take blocks of g labels. Each fixed ordered distinct pair lands in the same group with probability (g-1)/(N-1). The expected total added diagonal HS defect is exactly this factor times sum_{i!=j} ||K_ij||_HS^2 (by indicator linearity, no independence assumption). At least one partition attains no more than this expectation. A deterministic search for partitions using actual measured cross-block norms may improve the worst-case formula; the MUB example explains how. This is not a proof that the original theta-family cross-weights possess any analogous partition structure. Need actual Gram data or geometry, not arbitrary synthetic random matrices.

## C. Arithmetic compatibility obstruction: exact counterexample for grouping heights
The fixed metrics and heights in LinkDIV_at are part of the arithmetic construction. A grouped direct sum of integral maps is integral on the direct sum of shifted source lattices, but the required height bound does not follow from the original averaged bounds, even in the ideal Hilbert moment case. Let K=Q(sqrt(2),i), with two complex places represented by sqrt(2)->+/-sqrt(2), i->i. Let u=(1+sqrt(2))^n, n positive integer; this is an algebraic unit with |sigma_+(u)|=A>1, |sigma_-(u)|=1/A. Take N_1=N_2=O_K, target C=O_K^2, fixed standard metrics at both places. Integral maps B_1(v)=(uv,0), B_2(v)=(0,u^-1 v). Each map has averaged log-squared operator norm 0, whereas B_G=diag(u,u^-1) has squared operator norm A^2 at BOTH places, so h(B_G)=2log A, unbounded as n grows. At a chosen place, individually weighted normalized maps are exactly orthogonal unit coordinate inclusions: e=eta=0; group complex Gram can likewise be made identity using independent source coordinates Y_i and a grouped Y_G with block factors. Nevertheless at the fixed arithmetic X_i the height bound h(B_G)<=max_i h(B_i)+log2 FAILS for all n>=1. This is a rigorous number-field counterexample, not merely a numeric concern. The simpler scalar codomain test has local squared norms M+1/M and the same failure, as verified in the script.

If stronger *placewise* heights h_{i,w} are available, then ||B_G(w)||_op^2<=sum_i ||B_i(w)||_op^2<=|G| exp(max_i h_{i,w}), by Cauchy-Schwarz for horizontal concatenation, so h(B_G)<=log|G|+mean_w max_i h_{i,w}. This conditional bound cannot replace mean_w max_i by max_i mean_w. Orthogonal images give max rather than sum, but the two-place counterexample still applies. Adjusting metrics at each place can absorb individual local norms, but then changes lattice slopes and other arithmetic inputs; no preservation of LinkDIV_at asserted. Moreover source-specific ideals, source prime T_u and local corrections E_u depend on the label; a direct sum of distinct fractional-ideal shifts need not be a common shift of the original unshifted direct sum lattice. For example over a DVR, L=O^2 and shifted lattice pi^-1 O direct sum O cannot equal pi^-t L for any integer t (the two elementary divisors differ). Thus grouping cannot satisfy a single source-local valuation formula simply by relabeling.

Consequences: The actual proof's logarithmic label-count term is already dispensable in ARAK.1120's derivation (step 9 adds it after proving a stronger bound). Reducing labels is NOT a demonstrated way to reduce the arithmetic lift count, rank k or proof complexity. The obstacle has moved from abstract moment algebra to genuine period-family construction and preservation of the source height and local gain comparisons. No independent RH proof/disproof, no priority claim. No user machine or external repository modified.
