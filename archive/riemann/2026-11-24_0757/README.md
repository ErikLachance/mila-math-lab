## A. Sharp abstract simultaneous exceptional-exponent window
Source window theorem uses J=2^(R+2)(C/eta)^R for [Y,Y²], 0<eta<=C, X>1 and at most R nonnegative integer exceptional exponents e. For every x in interval require e<=x^C implies e<=x^eta. Generalize window to [Y,Y^a] for real a>1 and allow different eta_i,C_i for each exceptional exponent. A sufficient constant is

    J = a product_{i=1}^R (a C_i / eta_i).

In particular with common bounds and a=2, J=2^(R+1)(C/eta)^R, a factor 2 smaller than the published catalogue's sufficient J. This is an elementary independent sharpening of this lemma, not a claim of improvement to the full Lean theorem until all dependencies are rechecked.

Proof: e=0,1 automatically satisfy the implication since x>1. For each e>1 put b_i=log(e_i)/log X and y=log(Y)/log X. The implication fails somewhere on [Y,Y^a] exactly when b_i/(a C_i)<=y<b_i/eta_i. Indeed a witness log x/log X lies in [y,ay] intersected with [b_i/C_i,b_i/eta_i). This is half-open, and the upper endpoint is safe. If eta_i=C_i the original implication is always true, so that i may simply be dropped (the displayed forbidden-start interval is for eta_i<C_i only). In log(y) coordinates, each nontrivial forbidden interval has length log(a C_i/eta_i). The candidate interval [log(1/J),log(1/a)] has length exactly the sum of the budgets. If the finite union covered the closed candidate interval, some interval would also cover its right endpoint and extend strictly beyond it; hence union length would exceed the total budgets, impossible. There is a valid y. Equivalently pick an uncovered endpoint from the lower/upper candidates or the right endpoints of forbidden intervals. For R=0 take Y=X^(1/a). The actual exponent list permits choosing the largest safe y via merging intervals (boundary care: largest safe y might not exist if right bound covered and last allowed point is open; instead select ANY safe endpoint or interior). 

Sharpness for nontrivial common eta<C and R>=1: put B=aC/eta. For any J'<a B^R, J'>=a, the candidate log(y) interval has length strictly less than R log B. Choose R intervals each of length log B with consecutive strict overlaps, first extending strictly left and last strictly right of this interval, covering all candidates. Their positions specify positive b_i=aC times the left endpoints in y-coordinates. For arbitrarily large X choose integer e_i nearest X^(b_i); normalized log(e_i) approaches b_i. The strict overlaps and boundary margins persist, producing no valid Y. If J'<a there is no interval even without exponents. Thus a B^R is the optimal universal constant in this common, strictly nontrivial threshold setting. For mixed thresholds analogous covering works after removing trivial eta_i=C_i. Repeated exponents are harmless and may be merged; no assumed statistical independence or continuous relaxation of integer e required. Note the initial test program intentionally models all positive a_i as forbidden even when eta=C, which overconstrains the sufficient witness test; its sharpness models in that degenerate case are interval-model checks only, NOT counterexamples to the implication (which is tautological). This is explicitly corrected here to avoid a false claim of sharpness at eta=C.

Test: 18,000 exact rational normalized-log window cases, 16,035 strict-overlap interval sharpness models. These confirm the combinatorial mechanism, not the theorem for all real parameters (the proof above does that). Source GC.1.0105 uses J0=2^(R+2)(802 C_K/epsilon)^R. If the only use of the window constant is this lemma and the threshold ratio is strictly nontrivial, factor-two improvement could transfer; not yet checked in raw Lean. The exponent list and R must be fixed before choosing auxiliary primes as required by source, not fit retrospectively to a selected window.

## B. A polynomial bound for the negative localization contribution
Use exactly the negative eigenspace estimate stated in O'Desky's catalogue, not an independent derivation of the deep Bernoulli valuation hypothesis. Let o=ord(psi), N=conductor psi, t=floor(x^401), n=[E:Q], F=cyclotomic conductor of E. For x>=U>=2, field E is compositum of real 2^(nu+2)-cyclotomic layer of degree 2^nu, K_psi of degree o, and two quadratic fields. Thus n<=4o 2^nu<=8o x^(2+8epsilon), irrespective of q,d. Also F<=2^(nu+2)N qd, by conductor divisibility for abelian subfields. Source upper prime-size conditions imply log(qd)<=U^(epsilon/5)(1+log² U)<=x^(epsilon/5)(1+log² x). Hence

    log(t F) <= log(8N)+(403+8epsilon)log x + x^(epsilon/5)(1+log² x)
             <= A_psi x^(epsilon/5)(1+log x)^2

with a fixed positive A_psi; log(t ell F) obeys the same bound with A_psi+log ell. Insert into the catalogue negative sum bound 3 n t² log(tF)+C_- sum_{odd ell|o} ell n² t² log²(t ell F). The first term is O_psi,epsilon(x^(804+8epsilon+epsilon/5)(1+log x)^2); the second is O_psi,epsilon(x^(806+16epsilon+2epsilon/5)(1+log x)^4). Since epsilon<1/52, both exponents <807. Therefore the entire negative contribution is O_psi,epsilon(x^807(1+log x)^4), uniformly in q,d and the finite prime set. This is a polynomial rather than the coarse stretched-exponential absorption in the earlier catalogue derivation. If o is a power of 2 the second term is absent and exponent <805 suffices. Constants may depend on the fixed character and the original cohomological estimate; no concrete threshold asserted. The positive eigenspace and ell=2 still dominate; no stronger final RH/GRH result follows automatically. The crucial improvement is retaining n as a polynomial rather than replacing n by F, which is exponentially large in x^(epsilon/5).

## C. Integral involution defect and projection-lattice comparison
Let A be a finite Z_ell-module with c²=1, B+=ker(c-1), B-=ker(c+1). Let I=B+ intersect B-, Q=A/(B++B-). Exact elementary identities:

    |A| = |B+||im(c-1)|,
    |B+||B-|/|A| = |B-/im(c-1)| = |B+/im(c+1)|,
    length A = length B+ + length B- - length I + length Q.

Both I and Q are killed by 2 (2a=(a+ca)+(a-ca)). The map induced by c-1 identifies Q with im(c-1)/2B-, a subgroup of B-/2B-. Hence at ell=2 length Q<=length B-[2]=length I; length A<=length B++length B-, and the discrepancy is precisely the length of B-/im(c-1). Odd ell: exact split and equality. This holds also as a finiteness argument if A initially arbitrary but both eigenspaces finite, by A/B+ isomorphic to im(c-1) subset of B-. For A=Z/2 and c=id, the two lengths each equal 1, A length 1: adding them is not an exact equality. For c swapping coordinates on (Z/2)^2, I and Q both have length1 and the sum equality happens without direct splitting. These examples disprove naive extending the odd-prime splitting to ell=2; the source correctly uses the whole ell=2 kernel. Conditional upper bound could permit separate 2-primary eigenspace estimates, but no such arithmetic estimates proved here.

Related to GC.0106 congruence defects: let R a DVR, M a free finite R-lattice in V over fraction field, P an idempotent on V of rank r, complementary rank s. Set M1=M intersect im P, M0=M intersect ker P. The defect D=P(M)/M1 is canonically isomorphic to M/(M1+M0) by a map induced by P, and to (1-P)(M)/M0 by the complementary map. In particular complementary defects have equal length. If a P(M) subset M for nonzero a in R, D is annihilated by a, and generated by at most min(r,s) elements (both projected lattices are free of ranks r,s). Thus length_R D <= min(r,s) v_R(a). For an integral involution c, P=(1+c)/2 and a=2, so normalized defect (divide by ramification index) <= min(r,s) at residue characteristic 2 and zero at other primes. This does NOT bound the separate cohomology kernels; it controls a projection lattice when such an actual integral involution is available.

Sharp example R^2 ambient, M generated by e1+e2 and pi^k e2; coordinate projector P gives intersection M1=R pi^k e1, projected lattice R e1, defect R/(pi^k), and smallest denominator pi^k. Direct sums of independent pairs give all defect lengths sum k_i with largest denominator exponent max k_i and bound attained for all equal k_i. Extra pure eigenlines do not increase defect. In applications with a commutative integral algebra and h in that algebra separating selected and unselected eigenvalues, polynomials f,g with roots the distinct eigenvalues in opposite groups are coprime over the fraction field; resultant Res(f,g) is in R when eigenvalues lie in R. Bezout with coefficients in R after multiplying by resultant yields an integral operator Res(f,g)P (evaluate at h). Thus the defect is bounded by min(r,s) times valuation of the cross resultant. This is a conditional alternative denominator bound only; it may be enormous, and coincident eigenvalues across groups prohibit this use of a single h. Even arbitrarily close eigenvalues can have integral projection if the lattice already splits, so no matching lower bound based solely on eigenvalue gaps. Need actual source eigenvalue data, not synthetic rank estimates. ## D. Isolating p-adic twist resonances in a split rank-one model
Motivation: Lim Proposition 5.1 is for a FIXED twist and FIXED extension; its O(1) is not automatically uniform over r<t(x) or varying q,d. The following independent elementary lemma clarifies which part of a split model can be uniform.

Let p prime, b=1 if p odd and b=2 if p=2, g=1+p^b, u in 1+p^b Z_p. For r=1,...,T with T>=2, put v_r=v_p(u g^r-1), infinity allowed. By the elementary lifting-the-exponent identity v_p(g^m-1)=b+v_p(m) for nonzero integer m (binomial argument; at 2 requires g≡1 mod4). For k>b, all r with v_r>=k are in a single residue class modulo p^(k-b), because ratios of two such units give g^(r-s)≡1 mod p^k. Select one maximal r*. It belongs to every nonempty threshold set, including any infinite valuation. After deleting it, the count of v_r>=k is at most floor((T-1)/p^(k-b)). In particular every remaining v_r<=b+floor(log_p(T-1)), and summing threshold counts gives

    sum_{r != r*} v_r <= b(T-1)+(T-1)/(p-1).

This remains true for truncated valuations v'_r=min(v_r,K), selecting a maximal truncated value. For a diagonal model with d eigenvalues u_i all in 1+p^b Z_p, at most d exceptional indices (union of maximizers) leave a total valuation sum over the other indices at most d[b(T-1)+(T-1)/(p-1)]. Coinciding maximizers reduce their number, but no independence is assumed. For a fixed nonresonant scalar w, v_p(w^(p^n)-1)=v_p(w-1)+n. At p=2 the congruence mod4 is essential; e.g. w=3 has v2(3²-1)=3, not v2(3-1)+1=2.

Adversarial limitation: even rank one with fixed p and T admits arbitrarily large maximal valuation by u=g^(-r*) (1+p^H) for any H>=b; at r* it is H. Exact resonance u=g^(-r*) gives infinity. Bounded rank or zero Iwasawa mu alone cannot establish a uniform bound on these exceptional sizes; independent cohomological estimate is needed, which explains the importance of the actual exceptional-exponent window machinery. Actual modules may have nonsplit eigenvalues, nonfree torsion, other residue characters, comparison kernel/cokernel, and varying base fields; the lemma is not a substitute for GC.0086. No numerics used as proof.
