## A. Nonvanishing of trace gap without full Ramanujan (conditional normalization audit)
Let Q be a positive integer residue cardinality >=13, e>=1, |u|=1. Put a=Q^e. For the target t=1+a*u+a^(-1)*u^(-1), reverse triangle gives |t|>=a-1-a^(-1)>a-2>=Q-2>3 sqrt(Q). Last inequality holds since Q>=13, (13-2)^2=121>117, and Q-2-3sqrt(Q) is increasing there. For three normalized eigenvalues of modulus <sqrt(Q), the trace has modulus <3sqrt(Q), so the gap cannot vanish. This requires the trace to be in the normalization giving that bound; check inverse Frobenius convention as necessary (both upper and lower Satake bounds allow inversion), and ensure all selected points have good local comparison at the chosen prime. For rank-two plus a trivial character, cancellation of the 1 gives |a*u+a^(-1)*u^(-1)|>=a-a^(-1)>2sqrt(Q) for Q>=5, so rank-two normalized trace is likewise separated. No GRH or temperedness assumption needed. If comparing a rank-three trace to an unnormalized target, none of these conclusions is automatic.
In case I fix two distinct rational primes p,p' >=13 splitting in K (Dirichlet/Chebotarev), coprime to fixed 30 Npsi disc K; require q,d exceed p,p' as in the previous checkpoint. For each coefficient prime ell select p except ell=p, where p' is used. The trace criterion only applies if these places are indeed unramified and its local comparison holds at each selected point; this is a source-matching obligation rather than a theorem established here. The choice of p,p' precedes the auxiliary primes and twist, avoiding circularity. In the rank-two-plus-one case an even smaller prime works, but keeping a single fixed pair is simpler.

## B. A precise two-prime norm lemma, with necessary conditions
Let I be a common finite list of algebraic trace-gap packets indexed independently of ell, each in a number field F_i. For each p in {p,p'} and each i let a_{i,p} be nonzero with p^{D_{i,p}}a_{i,p} integral at all finite places, D>=0 integer. Suppose each complex embedding has |a_{i,p}|<=M_{i,p} and M>0. For ell!=p, all valuations of a above ell are nonnegative. For v normalized by v_ell(ell)=1, sum over all embeddings F_i->Qbar_ell v_ell(a) equals v_ell(Norm_{F_i/Q}a), because the norm is the product of the embeddings. Equivalently each prime w over ell contributes f_w ord_w(a); do not multiply this by e_w again. At ell!=p clearing the denominator has no effect on valuations. Thus for any selection of distinct pairs (i, embedding) at ell, its nonnegative sum is <= sum_i v_ell(Norm(p^D a)). Summing weighted by log ell over an arbitrary finite set excluding p yields <= sum_i log |Norm(p^D a)| <= sum_i [F_i:Q]*(D log p+log M). Split the coefficient-prime set into ell!=p and ell=p, using the second auxiliary prime for the latter. The sum is bounded by the two corresponding global budgets. This proves a sufficient arithmetic step for AGR.1123; it is not enough to prove the deformation point selection, the uniform cardinality of the list, or its arithmetic realization.
Equivalent determinant form: a finite etale Q-algebra A=product_i F_i and a lattice in A invariant under multiplication by b_p=(p^{D_i}a_i)_i give a nonzero integral determinant of that operator, equal to the product of norms. If its dimension is d and all complex eigenvalue moduli <=B then log|det|<=d log B. This can eliminate explicit number-field embeddings if a common integral Hecke lattice for the trace-gap operator is constructed. One needs actual distinct packet-to-embedding injection (or bounded multiplicity) and common operator. Qualitative finiteness from AGR.0316 alone does NOT supply polynomial degree-weighted size. If d<=C(N(e+1))^A and log B<=C'(e+1)log p, then the norm budget is polynomial of the required form (increasing A to absorb e+1). For a character kernel H, its faithful characters number phi([G:H]); one may sum separate packets if they are genuinely uniform in that kernel class. Still missing: construct these lattices at bounded level, prove conjugate-uniform archimedean bounds, bound denominator exponents uniformly, identify points with packets across coefficient primes, and preserve the phi factor.

### Adversarial counterexamples
1. Positive valuation at one embedding alone cannot be charged to a norm if other embeddings have negative valuation at the same ell. In Q(i), a=(2+i)/(2-i) has norm 1, yet the two places over 5 have valuations +1 and -1. Nonnegativity at all places over ell is essential.
2. Bounded archimedean size and integrality outside p do not imply a bounded prime budget. For each odd prime ell, a_ell=(2^(ell-1)-1)/2^(ell-1) lies in (0,1), with only 2 in its denominator, yet ell divides its numerator by Fermat. Even for fixed p=2 and degree 1, selecting new a_ell separately can make the sum arbitrarily large. Need denominator bound AND uniform packet list. A common list is also required to avoid counting an arbitrarily changing finite set from the primary AGR.1123 theorem as though it were fixed.
3. Merely extending coefficient rings does not multiply normalized lengths: for finite DVR extension O'/O, finite free base change commutes with continuous cochains on discrete coefficients (finite free direct sum), their cohomology and the kernel of the entire family of restrictions. Each cyclic O/varpi^j acquires length multiplied by e(O'/O), as does length(O'/ell). Thus normalized lengths invariant, subject to action and restriction maps being exactly base changed. This is not a statement that characters not defined over O were already present; enlarged coefficient fields can add characters, whose nonnegative terms are included only in an upper bound.

## C. Self-twist proof and consequence for descent cases
Independent short reconstruction of REP.1261 (known theorem, novelty not claimed). Let V be a nonzero irreducible finite-dimensional G-representation over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field. H normal and G/H abelian. Restriction to H is semisimple: select an irreducible H-submodule W, then sum all G-translates; they are simple H-modules, their sum is semisimple and by G-irreducibility equals V. If restriction reducible, End_H(V) has dimension >1. Conjugation by G on it factors through G/H. Trace-zero subspace is invariant and nonzero (characteristic zero splits off scalar identity); it has no nonzero G-fixed vector by Schur. Any commuting family of endomorphisms on a finite-dimensional space over an algebraically closed field has a common eigenvector (induction on dimension, taking a proper eigenspace for any nonscalar operator). Thus there exists nonzero T in the trace-zero subspace and a character chi with rho(g)T rho(g)^(-1)=chi(g)T. Chi cannot be trivial, is trivial on H, and T is an intertwiner between irreducible representations, hence invertible. Taking determinants shows chi(g)^dimV=1. In a continuous l-adic setting chi is continuous from a nonzero matrix coordinate of the conjugation equation. In particular, if G/H is finite of order coprime to dimV, restriction stays irreducible; rank three stays irreducible under quadratic descent. Rank two can split; when it does over a quadratic normal subgroup, trace vanishes on its nontrivial coset by conjugation-invariance of trace and chi=-1. This is the precise obstruction to assuming all rank-two restrictions remain irreducible. Explicit clock/shift representations give nontrivial examples for dimensions m, and their self twists were verified exactly below.
For case II of GC.0079, phi* has even order prime to ell, hence its half-order power is a nontrivial quadratic character, trivial on ell-primary inertia by construction. The quadratic field can therefore serve as a descent field unramified at ell. For case III and Z the character has odd order or is trivial, hence phi(-1)=1 and the parity condition forces r even. These are useful structural reductions but do not supply bounds for the Selmer groups or show that the source uses precisely these steps. In particular no assertion of vanishing from the mere existence of a quadratic field is justified.
