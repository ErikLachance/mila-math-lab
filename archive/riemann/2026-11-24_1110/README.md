## A. Exact character-wise descent at primes not dividing field degree; controlled defect otherwise
Let E/Q be the abelian CM field in GC.0079, G=Gal(E/Q), n=|G|, and let N be a cyclotomic modulus of E. Fix an odd prime p, integer r>=1, a DVR O finite over Z_p containing all character values of G, fraction field K, ramification index e=v_O(p). Let A=K/O with action chi_cyc^(-r)*phi^(-1), phi:G->O^*. Over E this is the pure cyclotomic negative twist. Write S_O(E) for the O-coefficient localization kernel and S_phi for its phi-eigenspace under the natural G action on the untwisted-character coefficient version. Define V_phi over Q by requiring classes in H1(G_Q,A) to vanish at every inertia group and at decomposition groups for primes dividing pN. This is precisely the strict local group underlying GC.0079 (before division by e).

First, S_O(E) can equivalently be described as classes locally zero at EVERY finite place of E. At w not above p a class is unramified. Inflation-restriction identifies its unramified local class with H1(procyclic residue Galois group,A) = A/(Frob-1)A, since the pure cyclotomic twist is unramified there. This quotient is zero: Frob acts by (Nw)^(-r), not 1 for r>=1, and multiplication by a nonzero element of O on K/O is surjective. At w above p vanishing is imposed. Conversely local zero implies unramified everywhere outside p and gives a class of G_{E,S_p} by inflation (inertia there acts trivially on coefficients). At archimedean places the relevant H1 vanishes for p odd. This observation fails for r=0 with trivial character, and fails for arbitrary finite coefficients (divisibility is needed).

Global inflation-restriction maps H1(G_Q,A) to H1(G_E,A)^G, with kernel H1(G,A^{G_E}) and cokernel embedded in H2(G,A^{G_E}). Twisting the conjugation action by phi^(-1) makes the invariant target exactly the natural phi-eigenspace, not the inverse eigenspace. Let W be the preimage of S_phi. At every rational prime v, the restriction of W to local E_w is zero by the preceding observation, so its local class lies in the inflation of H1(D_v,A^{G_{E_w}}), D_v a decomposition subgroup of G. At v not dividing pN, E/Q is unramified and A is unramified. Inertia groups are identical over Q_v and E_w, hence the local restriction to inertia is zero, and the unramified local quotient of A is zero because v^(-r)*phi(Frob)^(-1) is not 1 (a positive rational integer power >1 cannot be a root of unity). Therefore W is automatically locally zero outside pN. V_phi is exactly the kernel on W of the finitely many local maps at v|pN; its restriction to S_phi is well-defined.

In this specific E, the odd p-Sylow of G is CYCLIC: restriction injects G into Gal(real 2-power cyclotomic) x Gal(K_psi) x C2 x C2, the middle factor cyclic of order o=ord psi. Thus the odd p-Sylow has order dividing p^v_p(o). Same property for every D_v. Each A^H is an O-submodule of K/O, hence uniserial (cyclic finite or all K/O). For any finite abelian group D with cyclic p-Sylow P, averaging over its prime-to-p factor gives H^i(D,B)=H^i(P,B^{D_{p'}}) for p-primary coefficients B. This follows from exactness of the invariants functor for the prime-to-p factor and the inflation-restriction/higher cohomology sequence. The cyclic cohomology formulas H1=ker Norm/(g-1)B and H2=B^P/Norm B are subquotients of a uniserial module and killed by |P|. Thus each has O-length <=e v_p(|D|), independently of how large the invariant coefficient group B may be. A subquotient of K/O killed by p^a has length <=ea (if B finite, cyclic and killed; if B=K/O, any proper quotient of K/O is divisible, so a killed quotient must be zero). This handles also B=0.

Set a=v_p(n), s=number of distinct primes dividing pN. The kernel of V_phi -> S_phi has length <= ea. The cokernel has length <= e a(1+s): transgression contributes at most ea; imposing local vanishing costs at most the sum of s local H1 lengths, each <=ea. For the latter, W/(V_phi+ker res) is a quotient of W/V_phi, which embeds in the product of the finite local H1 groups. Therefore if V_phi has finite length then S_phi has finite length and
  len_O S_phi / e <= len_O V_phi / e + a(1+s).
If p does not divide n, the map is an ISOMORPHISM. No conductor- or r-dependent power of the coefficient ramification index remains after normalization. This is a derived explicit character-transfer bound, not a claim of literature novelty. Check signs: natural complex conjugation in S_O(E) includes the cyclotomic coefficient action, so the positive eigenspace in the catalogue is eigenvalue (-1)^r; exactly characters with phi(-1)=(-1)^r contribute.

## B. Integral eigenspace upper bound WITHOUT semisimplicity
Let M be an arbitrary O-module with an action of a finite abelian group G, O containing all roots needed for its characters. For a cyclic factor with generator T of order m, the identity product_{zeta^m=1}(T-zeta)=0 holds over O; even if distinct roots coincide modulo the uniformizer, the identity is a polynomial identity in O[T]. For two endomorphisms A,B, the sequence 0 -> ker B -> ker(AB) -> ker A via B is exact at the first two positions. Hence if the latter two kernel lengths are finite, len ker(AB)<=len ker A+len ker B. Induction on factors gives len M<=sum_zeta len ker(T-zeta), with finiteness of M following from finiteness of all individual kernels. Iterate over cyclic factors of the abelian group, each preserving the preceding kernels, to obtain
  len_O M <= sum_{phi in G^vee} len_O M_phi.
This applies to M=S_O(E)^+ without assuming M finite beforehand; eigenspaces with wrong parity vanish since 2 is a unit. It needs no division by n and no generator bound. If p does not divide n the usual idempotents give equality. For bad primes equality is false: take O=Z_3[zeta_3], M=O/(zeta_3-1) with trivial C3 action. All three character eigenspaces equal M, so their total length is 3 while actual length is 1. This counters both exact eigenspace splitting and the idea that pairwise eigenspaces have zero intersection at ramified primes. For upper bounds, the overcount is safe.

Flat base extension O over Z_p commutes with continuous cochains here (O finite free, finite direct sums), kernels and local maps, so S_O(E)=O tensor S_{p,r}(E), and normalized length is len_Zp S_{p,r}(E). Combine A and B. The odd-prime positive contribution is bounded by the normalized character lengths plus an explicit arithmetic correction supported ONLY on odd p|o. There are at most n characters, s<=omega(N_psi)+4 (N=lcm(2^(nu+2),N_psi) qd), a<=v_p(o). Thus for any finite prime set and 1<=r<t the cumulative weighted defect is at most
  n(t-1)(omega(N_psi)+5) log(o_odd),
where o_odd is the odd part of o. It is zero if o is a power of 2. This isolates the non-semisimple transfer error as a polynomial term, not a new analytic assumption. It does NOT prove the five character estimates in GC.0079 or explain all exceptional character families. They remain the major task. Avoid substituting this descent alone for automorphic input.

## C. Fixed odd-prime tower bound, conditional on supplied cohomological inputs
For fixed p in B_psi, L=Q(mu_{p N_psi}) contains mu_p and is totally imaginary. Class-group rank bound GC.0081 gives a uniform upper bound R on p-ranks of finite cyclotomic layers. The unramified, p-split dual module X of the infinite tower can be generated by at most R elements: any finite elementary abelian quotient corresponds by class field theory to a finite abelian exponent-p extension of L_infty unramified everywhere and split at p. It descends to a finite Galois extension at some finite layer with the same group: choose finite defining data, then enlarge the layer until the finite Galois group stabilizes (finite quotient, intersection stabilizes). Away from p the tower is unramified, so inertia is unchanged and unramifiedness descends. Above p, the number of primes stabilizes because local cyclotomic images are open in Z_p; the finite local decomposition images form descending finite groups with trivial intersection, hence eventually become trivial at all p-primes. Consequently the finite elementary abelian quotient is a quotient of the p-class group of a sufficiently high finite layer, with rank <=R. This bounds dim_Fp X/pX (if greater, a finite quotient of larger rank exists by Pontryagin duality). Compact pro-p Nakayama: lifts of a basis generate a closed subgroup with quotient Q satisfying pQ=Q; a nonzero pro-p compact abelian quotient has a nonzero finite p-group quotient, incompatible with p-surjectivity. Therefore X is finitely generated over Z_p. This descent uses standard class field theory; the exact raw formalization not read.

The preceding checkpoint A applies to L and its layers, so S_{p,r}(L_j)^dual is the cokernel of (u^r Gamma)^(p^j)-1. The layer depth j=floor(log_p R)+3 exceeds the free-lift depth s=ceil(log_p n_X) for n_X>0. Apply the earlier trimmed prefix bound with T=floor(X^401), M=t(x)-1: outside at most n_X exceptional twists, aggregate length <= n_X[(v_p(u-1)+j)M+M/(p-1)+floor(log_p(T-1))]. This is O(x^401+log x), with constants depending only on psi and p, because j and p fixed and X<=x^J. The small exceptional twists are handled by the finite-to-compact H2 comparison of previous checkpoint applied at this fixed layer L_j (degree/discriminant fixed). Under its hypotheses, lengths <= exp(O(r^C_K)) with constant independent of x. For r<=x^eta, choose eta C_K<=epsilon/4; hence exp(O(x^(epsilon/4))) is o(exp(x^(4epsilon/5))). For twists beyond M no contribution; the simultaneous window must avoid intermediate exceptional twists for ALL finitely many p along with the two-adic ones. This is a conditional reconstruction of odd GC.0086, not a proof of the class-group theorem, H2 input, or window existence. Fixed p conductor and j are chosen before q,d, so no circularity. If n_X=0, S is zero by the exact descent. Distinguish this fixed-layer result from controlling all odd primes through GC.0079.

## D. Sharper negative contribution from the actual degree
GC.0087 gives a conditional weighted negative sum <=3 n t^2 log(t F_E) + C_- sum_{p|o, p odd} p n^2 t^2 log(t p F_E)^2. The catalogue proof bounds n by conductor F_E, losing an exponential. But actual degree is <=4 o 2^nu <=8 o x^(2+8epsilon). Use t<=x^401, log(qd)<=x^(epsilon/4), and log F_E<=log(4*2^nu*N_psi*qd). Then log(t p F_E)=O(x^(epsilon/4)) for fixed p|o and sufficiently large x. Consequently the negative term is bounded by O(x^(804+33epsilon/4)+x^(806+33epsilon/2)). If o has no odd prime factor, the second term is absent. This is a genuine polynomial upper bound from the stated GC.0087 estimate, stronger than the coarse exp(8 x^(epsilon/4)) bound used in GC.1.0105 exposition, but does not change the dominant positive/cyclotomic error. No claim the authors did not know this simplification. It cannot remove the need for Bernoulli eigenspace estimates.
