## Pass A — remove prime-power terms rigorously and identify their bias
Let k(v)=(1-|v|)_+, K(s)=(sinh(s/2)/(s/2))^2, A=K(1/2), t=log x. Previous centered discrepancy B(t)=A exp(t/2)-sum_{n>=2} Lambda(n)n^(-1/2) k(log n-t). Define the prime-only discrepancy P(t)=A exp(t/2)-sum_{p} log p/sqrt(p) k(log p-t). Then P=B+sum_{r>=2} S_r(t), S_r(t)=sum_p log p*p^(-r/2) k(r log p-t).
For fixed r, Stieltjes integrate against theta(u), supported u in [exp((t-1)/r),exp((t+1)/r)]. Replace d theta(u) by du, giving exactly exp((1/r-1/2)t) K(1/r-1/2)/r via v=r log u-t. For the error, integrate by parts against theta(u)-u; the weight is continuous, piecewise C1, zero at endpoints, derivative bounded O_r(u^(-r/2-1)). Thus the unconditional PNT gives error O_r(exp((1/r-1/2)t) exp(-c_r sqrt(t))) for t large. In particular S_2=1/2+O(exp(-c_2 sqrt(t))). Chebyshev alone yields for each r>=3, uniformly r<= (t+1)/log2, S_r<=C exp((t+1)/r-(t-1)/2). For r>=3 the sum is O((t+1)exp(-t/6)); sharpen by treating r=3 separately and r>=4 giving O(exp(-t/6)+t exp(-t/4))=O(exp(-t/6)). Hence P(t)-B(t)-1/2 is absolutely integrable on [2,infinity). Under RH the usual theta error O(sqrt(u) log^2 u) gives square-term error O(t^2 exp(-t/4)), so in that case the cube term's leading coefficient K(-1/6)/3 at exp(-t/6) is justified. Unconditionally the square error can dominate the cube term; do NOT assert a two-term asymptotic unconditionally.

## Pass B — a prime-only, logarithmically integrated RH criterion
F(T)=integral_2^T (P(t)-1/2)dt for T>=2, extend by zero on [0,2]. Define triangular CDF H(v)=0 for v<=-1, (1+v)^2/2 for -1<=v<=0, 1-(1-v)^2/2 for 0<=v<=1, 1 thereafter. Exact finite arithmetic identity:
 F(T)=2A(exp(T/2)-e) - sum_{p<=exp(T+1)} log(p)/sqrt(p) [H(T-log p)-H(2-log p)] -(T-2)/2.
The Laplace transform of F is the transform of (P-1/2)1_{t>=2}, divided by s, initially Re s>1/2. From prior Mellin formula it extends meromorphically to Re s>0 with nonzero simple-pole residues m_rho*K(z)/z at z=rho-.5 with Re z>0; prime-power correction holomorphic there (bounded, even L1 after centering). No real poles in Re s>0 for zeta. RH implies B(t)=sum_rho K(i gamma) exp(i gamma t)+O(exp(-5t/2)) by explicit formula for t>=2. The series converges absolutely/uniformly since K(i gamma)=O((1+|gamma|)^-2), N(T)=O(T log T); no nontrivial real zero of zeta, discreteness gives positive minimum |gamma|, so the integrated series is uniformly bounded. Therefore F=O(1) under RH, using L1 correction from A. Conversely F=O(1) implies Laplace transform holomorphic Re s>0 and thus RH by pole preservation. Even one-sided F<=C or F>=-C suffices, by Landau positivity applied to C-F or C+F (as earlier checkpoint). Consequence: RH equivalent to existence of finite limit of average (1/T) integral_2^T P(t)dt; if such limit exists, F=O(T), so transform holomorphic, and limit must be 1/2. No proof of the bounded primitive itself. Note distinction from preceding arithmetic sample average weighted by y rather than log x.

## Pass C — GRH via only the real part of a prime-only sum, one-sided bound
For a fixed primitive nonprincipal character chi, B_chi(t)=-sum Lambda(n)chi(n)/sqrt(n) k(log n-t), and P_chi(t)=-sum_p log p chi(p)/sqrt(p) k(log p-t). No pole main term. Chebyshev gives P_chi-B_chi=O(1) since higher powers bounded in absolute value by A's estimates. Its primitive difference is O(T), without needing uniform PNT in conductor. Put F_chi(T)=integral_2^T Re P_chi(t)dt, extended by zero. Transform in Re s>0 has poles at centered zeros of either L(s+.5,chi) or its conjugate character, residues K(z)*(m_chi(z)+m_conj(z))/(2z) (sum multiplicities, absent zeros count 0). This follows by transforming Re B_chi as (B_chi+B_conj)/2 and the same absolutely convergent Euler derivative calculation. No cancellation even at coincident zeros. At a real positive pole z the residue is STRICTLY POSITIVE, K(z)>0. Under GRH for chi, functional equation/conjugation ensure GRH for conjugate, explicit formula has bounded B_chi (a possible central zero contributes a constant, not necessarily zero), so F_chi=O(T). Conversely if F_chi<=C(1+T) eventually, increase C to cover initial values, g=C(1+T)-F_chi>=0. Its Laplace transform is meromorphic on Re s>0 with the same poles but negated residues. If g has positive real abscissa of convergence, Landau forces a real singularity there; this is necessarily a simple real pole with negative residue, so transform tends to negative infinity from the right, impossible for a nonnegative integrand. If no positive abscissa, transform holomorphic Re s>0, also impossible if any such zero exists. Functional equation and conjugation handle the left half. Thus a one-sided O(T) bound on the REAL part's integrated prime sum is equivalent to GRH for this fixed chi. A lower bound alone does not rule out a hypothetical real zero (positive residue yields positive exponential growth). This is a distinct obstacle from zeta; never silently assume absence of Siegel-type real zeros. The stated criterion uses a fixed character, no uniform-in-conductor assertion. Possible central zero prevents claiming F_chi=O(1) without removing its slope.

## Pass D — strengthen B/C to arbitrarily late proportional log windows, following the Waterloo analytic argument
The source pp.30–31 uses high derivatives of a Mellin transform to concentrate on a log interval and detect a pole via nearest singularities. Adapt that method to our PRIME-ONLY PRIMITIVES and allow positive real residues, as follows.
Lemma: Let real f(t), t>=0, locally integrable, |f(t)|<=C exp(a t), with transform L meromorphic Re s>0, all poles simple, Re pole<=a, and positive residues at real positive poles. For any fixed lambda>1 and 0<theta<a, if f(t)<=C_theta exp(theta t) throughout [U,lambda U] for arbitrarily large U, then L has no pole Re s>theta. If no real poles, the same proof works for -f; for positive real poles only upper bound is warranted.
Proof: Choose sigma>a sufficiently large. Let alpha=(1+lambda)/2, integer j nearest (sigma-a)alpha U, and n=j+1. For A_j(s)=(-1)^j L^(j)(s)/j!, the triangle inequality gives |A_j(sigma+iv)|+A_j(sigma)<=2 integral f_+(t) t^j exp(-sigma t)/j! dt, for every real v. Inside window bounded by 2 C_theta (sigma-theta)^(-n). Outside, divide by full gamma integral: gamma(shape n, rate sigma-a) has mean n/(sigma-a)~alpha U. Fix l in (1/alpha,1) and r in (1,lambda/alpha). For U large, outside window lies outside [l*mean,r*mean]. Chernoff bound from gamma MGF gives tail <=2 exp(-c n), c=min(l-1-log l,r-1-log r)>0. Choose sigma so log((sigma-theta)/(sigma-a))<c, giving whole bound O((sigma-theta)^(-n)) along sequence j->infinity. Differentiation justified by initial absolute convergence.
If a pole to right of theta exists, put R=min(sigma-theta, distance from sigma to nearest pole in Re s>0), treating absent nearest as infinity. Choose sigma avoiding equal distances between a real and a nonreal pole (countably many excluded sigma); conjugate pairs may still tie. If nearest pole is real with distance d<R+0 and d<sigma-theta, it is unique nearest, positive residue implies A_j(sigma)=c0*d^(-n)+O(r0^(-j)) for d<r0<min(sigma-theta,next pole distance), by Cauchy estimate after subtraction. At v=0, 2A_j(sigma) eventually exceeds bound, contradiction. Otherwise some nonreal pole has horizontal distance <R: if R=sigma-theta use alleged pole; if R<sigma-theta use closest nonreal pole, whose horizontal distance is strictly less than radial distance. Shift imaginary center to near its ordinate, perturb to avoid equal distances (locally finite set), obtaining unique nearest pole at d<R. Choose d<r0<R also below next distance. Disk lies in Re>theta, and Cauchy estimates after subtracting principal part imply |A_j(sigma+iv)|>=|c0|d^(-n)-O(r0^(-j)), while |A_j(sigma)|=O(r0^(-j)). Contradiction with bound. In cases of equality R=sigma-theta and a real pole at distance R, it need not be treated as nearest inside; alleged strictly-right pole gives horizontal distance<R. This proves lemma. No need maximal real part attained among all zeros, no global series interchange in continued domain.
Apply to F or F_chi: unconditional exponential order a can be any a>1/2 by Chebyshev. RH (or fixed-chi GRH) is equivalent to for every epsilon>0, arbitrarily large U with F(t)<=C_epsilon exp(epsilon t) on entire [U,lambda U], respectively F_chi. For RH sufficiency use nonreal-pole case; for chi real-pole positivity branch is essential. Under RH/GRH, O(1)/O(T) meets bounds. In x space windows are [Y,Y^lambda]. Constants may depend on lambda,epsilon,chi but not U. The result is not a claim that arbitrarily sparse isolated sample points work; a whole proportional log interval is essential to this proof. It transfers an existing technique, no priority claim. It weakens the arithmetic target, still not solved.
