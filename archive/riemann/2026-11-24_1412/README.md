## A. Sharper local separation including source's p>=7 range

For rank three assume normalized eigenvalues beta_j satisfy q^{-1/2}<=|beta_j|<=q^{1/2} and product of moduli =1. The latter follows for unramified unitary central character in the usual normalization; verify the actual source's unitary and local comparison hypotheses before application. Write x_j=log|beta_j|/log(sqrt q). The polytope -1<=x_j<=1, sum x_j=0 has vertices the permutations of (1,0,-1): two independent active coordinate inequalities needed at a vertex, same-sign pair impossible. Convex function sum exp(x_j log sqrt q) is bounded by its maximum at a vertex. Therefore |sum beta_j|<=sqrt q+1+1/sqrt q. ## B. Resultant certificate with multiplicity and pollution removal
A precise sufficient condition for hypothesis T that avoids construction of a common integral Hecke algebra. Fix K,e,N,p,w, q=p^f. Suppose a monic P(X) in Z[X] has, among its roots, all p^D times eligible trace values for some integer D>=fe; its root multiplicity at each trace dominates the number of pairwise nonisomorphic eligible representations with that trace under each comparison isomorphism. Assume the actual roots and all their Galois conjugates have modulus <=p^D B, and each target differs from every actual trace. Let d=deg P. Use the product of all monic irreducible factors of P whose roots all have modulus <=p^D B, retaining original multiplicities; call it P_small. This is integral by Gauss lemma. It still contains every needed trace root to required multiplicity. It may still include extraneous roots equal to targets. Let Q(X)=product over i in I (X-p^D t_i), t_i=q^{-e}z_i+1+q^e z_i^{-1}. Q is monic integral since roots are algebraic integers (D>=fe), and Galois stability of the family including repetitions makes its coefficients rational. Delete from P_small ALL copies of each common irreducible factor with Q. Call the resulting P_good, monic integral and coprime to Q. No actual root was deleted: if its irreducible factor had a target root, Galois stability of targets would force it itself to be a target root. This also follows from the nonvanishing assumption for all conjugates. Define Delta=abs(Res(Q,P_good)) (1 if empty). This nonzero integer is independent of ell and the chosen comparison isomorphisms.
For every ell != p all roots of both polynomials are ell-integral, and scaling p^D is an ell-unit. Resultant product formula gives v_ell(Delta) as sum of nonnegative valuations of all pairs (target index, polynomial root with multiplicities). For each i, the chosen distinct representation traces are a submultiset by hypothesis, so their valuations sum to <= v_ell(Delta), even if representation trace values repeat or distinct i have the same target. This proves the uniform prime sum, rather than merely one-prime valuation estimates. Archimedean product bound gives log Delta <= |I| d [D log p+log(B+q^e+1+q^{-e})]. If d<=C_K (N(e+1))^A, D<=C_K(e+1), B<=q^{C_K(e+1)}, this has required form after increasing A. No multiplication by cyclotomic degree beyond |I|; do not replace multiset by distinct trace values. No simultaneous diagonalization over residue fields required; characteristic zero eigenvectors suffice to account multiplicity. Need actual uniformly bounded spectra and multiplicity injection, not yet established. A monic integral polynomial from full cohomology can be used even if it contains boundary/Eisenstein factors, PROVIDED actual trace roots are independently identified and bounded. One gcd division is insufficient when P has repeated factors. Removing all matching factors without proving noncollision can remove genuine roots and invalidate the bound. These are exact logical requirements.

## C. Uniform polynomial degree via finite-cover cohomology (conditional on level/weight coverage)
General lemma: fix a torsion-free arithmetic quotient having a finite CW model with C total cells, and a rank r local system V. For any finite cover of degree m, pull back the cell decomposition; each cell lifts m times. The sum of dimensions of all rational cohomology groups is at most C m r, since each cochain group has that bound degreewise. No assumption on torsion or integral splitting. For a fixed neat subgroup inside GL_n(O_K), intersecting it with the principal congruence subgroup of rational integer level M yields index <=|GL_n(O_K/M)|<=M^{[K:Q] n^2}, via reduction. The finite class/component count at the fixed initial level can be incorporated into C. For an imaginary quadratic field and rank three this gives M^{18}; for rank two over Q gives M^4. A finite CW model at fixed level is a standard Borel-Serre input, not independently established here. For weights lambda=(a,b,c), a>=b>=c with |a|,|b|,|c|<=C'(e+1), Weyl's formula dim V_lambda=(a-b+1)(a-c+2)(b-c+1)/2 gives O((e+1)^3). At two embeddings, tensor dimensions O((e+1)^6). If fixed finite arch weight types only, degree bound O(M^{18}(e+1)^6) for rank three, O(M^4(e+1)) for rank two over Q. If polynomially many types, add that exponent. If actual levels each <=L but need not divide one fixed M, use direct sum across M=1..L; total rank <=C L^{19}(e+1)^6 or C L^5(e+1) respectively. Taking lcm of levels up to L instead would inflate level exponentially. This resolves the elementary quantitative step under explicit coverage assumptions; actual conductor bound independent of ell, actual cohomological type, strong multiplicity one/trace matching and integral operator normalization are still missing. ## D. Alternative avoiding integral Hecke lattice: p-adic slopes
If a fixed automorphic packet has algebraic normalized trace values in a Galois-stable finite multiset, and compatible continuous Galois realizations for every coefficient prime ell != p at every embedding, those trace values are integral away from p. Proof: compact image of a profinite group has bounded matrices over a finite local coefficient field and preserves a full lattice by summing the orbit of a standard lattice (bounded inside a fixed dilation, contains original). Thus traces integral. Equivalently eigenvalues of an element have absolute value 1 by bounded powers in both directions.
At p, suppose its crystalline realization matches the unramified local polynomial (with correct twist and Frobenius convention), and normalized Hodge jumps lie in [-H,H] at every embedding. For a weakly admissible filtered phi-module, Newton polygon lies above Hodge polygon with equal endpoints. For sorted slopes n_1<=...<=n_d and Hodge slopes h_1<=...<=h_d, compare first partial sum to get n_1>=h_1, and compare partial sum at d-1 with total equality to get n_d<=h_d. Hence every normalized slope lies in [-H,H]. For K_w unramified of residue degree f, eigenvalues of phi^f have v_p in [-fH,fH]; inverse convention flips signs but preserves interval. When H<=C(e+1) and this holds for every coefficient embedding above p, multiplying the trace by p^{ceil(fH)} is sufficient for integrality at p. Increase D to >=fe for target integrality. Thus one can construct P directly from the Galois-stable trace multiset, without a common integral Hecke operator. The critical unproven coverage is that the family admits the matching crystalline p-realizations with bounded weights, and conjugate-stable trace multiplicities; in the source AGR.1.0011 has spherical comparison, but its exact interface and Hodge weights must be matched. Cannot infer a common family from a single compatible realization or use weak admissibility at noncrystalline places.
