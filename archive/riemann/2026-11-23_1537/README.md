## New research pass 2: falsification of a sparse-sample substitute in Landau/Mellin criterion

A generic model for x>1: `G(x)=x^(3/4) cos(3 log x)`. Its Mellin transform for Re(s)>3/4 is `(s-3/4)/((s-3/4)^2+9)`, with genuine meromorphic poles at `3/4 +/- 3i`. But on an unbounded sequence `x_k=exp((pi/2+2pi*k)/3)`, G(x_k)=0, so infinitely many arbitrarily large samples satisfy G(x_k)<=x_k^(3/5), even though poles remain to the right of 3/5. On the peak sequence `y_k=exp(2pi*k/3)`, `G(y_k)/y_k^(3/5)=exp((3/20)*(2pi*k/3))` tends to infinity; sufficiently long logarithmic windows [Y,Y^(3/2)] always contain peaks. Therefore checking a Landau-type small-error inequality only at isolated samples or sparse checkpoints is mathematically INSUFFICIENT; the original requirement over complete intervals must be preserved. This is a generic toy counterexample, not any counterexample to Riemann or the original source. `sparse_window_adversary.py/.json` ran at 60 dps; numerical Mellin check error ~2.25e-44.
