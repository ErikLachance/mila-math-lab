## A. Uniform torsion-inclusive presentation lemma
The catalogue GC.1298 states a uniform bound R on the number of Z_2 generators of two dual modules X_N(L), N=1,2, over L=M(i,sqrt(-q),sqrt(-d)), with q=3 mod8, d=7 mod16 and coprimality. This is stronger than a bound on the free rank alone and is exactly the sort of input needed below. Derive a purely algebraic bound from that input, not a proof of GC.1298.

Let p be prime, M a finitely generated Z_p module with n minimal generators, and Gamma an automorphism arising from a continuous Z_p action. Let pi:Z_p^n -> M be a surjection by minimal generators, so pi modulo p is an isomorphism. Lift each Gamma image of a generator to Z_p^n; the resulting A satisfies pi A=Gamma pi, and A mod p is invertible and unipotent: the continuous pro-p action on the finite F_p-vector space has p-power order, and in characteristic p the polynomial X^(p^k)-1=(X-1)^(p^k). Therefore A lies in GL_n(Z_p). No splitting of M, no finite-torsion exponent bound, no semisimplicity assumption. Let b=1 for odd p, b=2 for p=2, and s=ceil(log_p n)+(b-1) for n>=1. Then A^(p^s) is congruent to I modulo p^b, since a unipotent n by n matrix over F_p has p^ceil(log_p n)-th power identity; at p=2 an additional squaring sends I+2B to I+4(B+B^2).

Fix u in 1+p^b Z_p with v_p(u-1)=b (if actual cyclotomic generator has a deeper valuation b', replace b by b' in scalar valuation estimates, retaining the matrix power threshold b). For positive integer r and nonnegative h, set F_h(r)=(u^r A)^(p^h)-I, and f_h(r)=(u^r Gamma)^(p^h)-1. The surjection pi induces a surjection coker F_h(r) -> coker f_h(r). Whenever det F_h(r) !=0, length of the former is v_p(det F_h(r)) by Smith normal form over Z_p. Thus it gives an upper bound on the latter regardless of size of torsion in M. For h<s, F_s=F_h times a geometric sum, hence image F_s subset image F_h and coker F_s surjects onto coker F_h. Therefore one can always replace h by max(h,s) for the upper bound. n=0 gives M=0 and no exceptions.

## B. Nonsplit eigenvalues and a uniform trimmed-sum bound
Let alpha_1,...,alpha_n be eigenvalues of A^(p^s), counted with multiplicity, in a finite splitting extension of Q_p with valuation normalized v(p)=1. They satisfy v(alpha_i-1)>=b: the eigenvalues of the integral matrix (A^(p^s)-I)/p^b are integral, by its monic characteristic polynomial. They are units, and no diagonalizability is required for determinant identities. For each r=1,...,T with T>=2 let w_i(r)=v(alpha_i u^(r p^s)-1), allowing infinity. For distinct r,t the difference of the two expressions is a unit times u^(p^s(r-t))-1, with valuation b+s+v_p(r-t) by LTE. Ultrametric inequality implies that if both w_i values exceed a level b+s+k-1, the integer difference has v_p(r-t)>=k. In particular, at most one index has w_i(r)>b+s+floor(log_p(T-1)). Choose r_i maximizing w_i among 1..T (tie arbitrary). For every other r, w_i(r)<=b+s+v_p(r-r_i) by comparing with the maximal value. This inequality directly handles fractional valuations in ramified extensions and avoids requiring p-adic logarithm centers or split eigenvalues.

For each k>=1, the number of remaining r with p^k | r-r_i is floor((r_i-1)/p^k)+floor((T-r_i)/p^k)<=floor((T-1)/p^k). Therefore sum_{r != r_i} w_i(r)<=(b+s)(T-1)+(T-1)/(p-1). Let E_T={r_i:1<=i<=n}. It has <=n elements, independent of h. For r outside E_T, all w_i finite. For h>=s, LTE in the finite extension holds as v(alpha_i u^(r p^s)-1)>=b>1/(p-1); binomial expansion shows the linear term pz has strictly smaller valuation than all higher terms. Thus v(det F_h(r))=n(h-s)+sum_i w_i(r). This is valid for repeated roots and nontrivial Jordan blocks, as triangularization over splitting field is sufficient. With H=max(h,s), conclusion:

    length coker f_h(r) <= n [b+H+floor(log_p(T-1))], r outside E_T;

    sum_{r outside E_T} length coker f_h(r)
      <= n[(b+H)(T-1)+(T-1)/(p-1)].

The second uses at most T-1 nonexceptional indices when n>0 and h-s>=0. The aggregate bound is O(n T(h+log_p n+1)), removing the extra log T from a naive summation of pointwise bounds. It is a direct algebraic auxiliary theorem; no first-ever novelty claim. For an actual generator u of valuation b'>b replace scalar b in the final bound by b'; same reasoning, provided all quantities are in principal units. It is not yet shown that the actual localization kernels are bounded by these cokernels plus controlled errors, which needs the descent comparison and duality.

Adversarial failure checks: bounded n alone without pro-p action doesn't give the stated uniform power s. For p=2, skipping the extra squaring can invalidate LTE. For a finite module Z/p^a, Gamma=u^(-r0) makes the twisted map zero at r0, so coker length=a arbitrarily large with n=1; free lift determinant may vanish but finite target still has finite length. Thus exceptional twists cannot be dismissed or bounded by generator number. Removing all maxima is safe even when determinant singular. No claim of uniform exceptional sizes. The cohomological estimate for small exceptional twists remains necessary.

## C. Correct quantifier ordering for varying fields; no circular choice
GC.1.0105 explicitly fixes J and threshold, then X, U=X^(1/J), then q,d, then Y, then all x in [Y,Y^2]. The same q,d stay fixed while layer nu(x) varies. GC.1298 bounds n uniformly in q,d but the exceptional sets may depend on q,d; this is allowed because Y is chosen after the pair. The odd-prime fixed-base towers have their own finite lists. Union their lists for T=floor(X^401), then choose the window using the previous elementary covering lemma. This avoids an invalid stronger demand for a single finite exceptional list valid across all q,d. In an abstract family of rank-one modules Gamma_t=u^(-t), the union of resonance indices over t=1..T is all T, even though each module has exactly one resonance. Thus a family-uniform bound on list size does not imply a family-uniform list. The auxiliary-prime analytic estimate in catalogue ANT.1.0101 is over [U,U^A] with A chosen beforehand; taking A>=J contains the cohomological interval since Y>=U and Y^2<=X=U^J. This establishes compatibility of the abstract order, not the analytic theorem or actual class-group bound.
