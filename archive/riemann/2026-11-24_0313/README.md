## A. Improved finite-dimensional second-moment lemma (independent derivation)
Setup precisely matches the linear-algebra component of the catalogue's slope theorem. Normalize A_i=sqrt(gamma_i) Btilde_i, d_i=dim source, R=sum d_i>0, common complex target dimension D>0. Let Q=sum A_i A_i*, T=tr Q, e<1 satisfy sum ||A_i* A_i-I||_HS^2<=eR, and eta>=0 satisfy D tr(Q^2)<= (1+eta)T^2. T>0 because the trace deviations are bounded by sqrt(e) R. Choose arbitrary retained subspaces E_i, with total discarded dimension h and f=h/R. Write F_i for orthogonal projections onto discarded parts. Q_dis=sum A_i F_i A_i*, Q_keep=Q-Q_dis. Let q=tr(Q_dis)/T and z=codim(sum A_i E_i)/D.

Mass lemma: set H_i=A_i* A_i-I and normalized trace deviations a=(sum tr(F_i H_i))/R and b=(sum tr((I-F_i) H_i))/R. Then q=(f+a)/(1+a+b) and q-f=(1-q)a-q b. In the Hilbert-Schmidt direct sum, the coefficient matrices F_i and I-F_i are orthogonal. Cauchy-Schwarz gives
  (q-f)^2 <= e [ f(1-q)^2+(1-f)q^2 ] = e[(q-f)^2+f(1-f)].
Hence |q-f|<=sqrt(e f(1-f)/(1-e)), so q<=q_*(f)=min(1, f+sqrt(e f(1-f)/(1-e))). This improves the cruder separate trace bounds. No commuting assumption on H_i and F_i; trace against an orthogonal projection works regardless. Equality possible: two unit-coordinate source maps with squared norms 6/5 and 2/5, discard first. Then f=1/2, e=1/5, q=3/4, equality holds exactly. Thus one cannot universally replace the sqrt(e f(1-f)) by O(e) at fixed f.

Rank lemma: Q_keep is positive semidefinite and Q_keep<=Q. Thus tr(Q_keep^2)<=tr(Q^2) because tr(Q^2)-tr(Q_keep^2)=tr((Q-Q_keep)(Q+Q_keep))>=0 (trace of product of PSD operators, no commuting assumption). Rank-trace Cauchy gives (1-q)^2 <= (1-z)(1+eta), so z <= (eta+2q-q^2)/(1+eta).

Stronger complementary projection lemma: let P be orthogonal projection onto the complement of retained images, rank zD. P Q_keep=0. Thus tr(PQ)/T<=q. Center Q/T around I/D and P around zI: HS Cauchy gives |tr(PQ)/T-z|<=sqrt(eta z(1-z)). In particular z-q<=sqrt(eta z(1-z)). If z<=q_* the following bound is automatic (root>=q_*); if z>q_*, square and solve. Therefore
  z <= (2 q_* + eta + sqrt(eta^2+4 eta q_*(1-q_*)))/(2(1+eta)).
Also retain the rank lemma as an independent bound and take their minimum if useful. Both are valid for all complex matrices in the stated setting, with f=0 and f=1 included. When eta=e=0 the complementary bound yields z<=f exactly. When no dimensions discarded, z<=eta/(1+eta), independent of e. These are stronger than the catalogue's coarse bound z<=eta+4f+4sqrt(e) after capping at 1: q_*<=f+sqrt(e)/(2sqrt(1-e)); for e<=1/4 the rank bound alone is <=eta+2f+(2/sqrt(3))*sqrt(e). The complement bound in fact is <= the rank bound: root characterization or separate CS on complement of P (trace>=1-q); any feasible z in quadratic variance inequality with z>=q implies (1-q)^2<= (1-z)(1+eta), via the centered Cauchy bound. Algebraically for all q in [0,1], the rank upper bound is >= the root (square the difference, or note it lies to the right of vertex and satisfies quadratic nonnegative). Not relying on this comparison for validity.

## B. Consequence for the slope theorem's loss (conditional on its existing lattice hypotheses)

Its discarded fraction at threshold tau>=0 is f<=F=U/(U+tau) if U>0; if U=0 no discard. So q<=F+sqrt(e/(1-e) F(1-F)). Target rank r >=(1-delta)D, so the fraction of target slopes below s-tau is at most z/(1-delta). Use the rank-trace lemma dropping -q^2, and integrate over [0,S] just as in catalogue proof. For U>0,
  integral F = U log(1+S/U),
  integral sqrt(F(1-F)) = 2 sqrt(U S) - 2 U arctan sqrt(S/U).
Thus the independent refinement is
  s-mu(C) <= [eta S + 2U log(1+S/U) + 4 sqrt(e/(1-e)) (sqrt(U S)-U arctan sqrt(S/U))] / [(1+eta)(1-delta)].
When U=0 use eta S/[(1+eta)(1-delta)]. S=0 gives 0. The original log|I| term is unnecessary in this proof (already observed in the catalogue proof), since mean slope is controlled by lower tails.
