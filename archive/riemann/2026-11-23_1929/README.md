## B. New angle: explicit universal fixed bump for the Weil translation criterion
Primary paper Payam Danesh, A quantitative two-interval criterion for the Riemann hypothesis, arXiv:2611.10726, 7 Nov 2026, actual article text retrieved at https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10726 (Lemma 1–3, Theorem 4, Corollary 5, §5). HTML alternate route returned DisabledError; abs page supplied full rendered article. Danesh proves equivalence using all localized pairs of subexponentially shrinking widths and a Laplace pole argument. The following self-contained specialization/strengthening of the choice of probe is derived here, with no claim of novelty over literature.

Let a_j=2^(−j), j≥1. Let U_j independent uniform variables on [−a_j,a_j] and µ the distribution of sum U_j; absolute convergence, support [−1,1]. H(z)=∫exp(zx)dµ(x)=product_{j≥1} sinh(a_j z)/(a_j z), with factors continued at zero. Uniform convergence on every compact follows from sum a_j²<∞ and |sinh(w)/w−1|≤|w|² for |w|≤1. For Re z≠0 no factor vanishes (all nonzero zeros of sinh are purely imaginary). An absolutely convergent product of nonzero factors with summable |factor−1| is nonzero; hence H detects EVERY off-critical-line centered zeta zero, not merely a separately chosen bump per zero. On the imaginary axis, all factors ≤1 in modulus, and each of the first k factors ≤min(1,1/(a_j |y|)); hence |H(iy)|≤C_k(1+|y|)^(−k) for every k. Fourier inversion of the finite probability measure gives a smooth density h (all derivatives exist by integrable Fourier moments); h is real, nonnegative, even, supported in [−1,1], integral 1. Thus h∈C_c^∞. Uniform rapid decay of H on |Re z|≤1/2 follows by integration by parts of h. Critical-line zeros might be annihilated by H; no issue, since only off-line zeros must be detected. This is not a proof of RH or a claim that H has no imaginary-axis zeros.

Define F(t)=B(τ_t h,h) with Danesh's centered Weil form, where z_ρ=ρ−1/2. Even real h gives F(t)=Σ_ρ H(z_ρ)^2 exp(t z_ρ). It is real by conjugation symmetry. Absolute convergence on compact t intervals and C=Σ|H(z_ρ)|²<∞ follow from the zero-counting bound N(T)=O(T log T) and rapid decay. For t≥0, |F(t)|≤C exp(b t), b=sup Re z_ρ∈[0,1/2]. Initially Re s>1/2, Laplace transform is Σ H(z_ρ)^2/(s−z_ρ), using absolute integrability bound C/(Re s−1/2). The sum is meromorphic on C by locally uniform tail estimates away from discrete poles, with residue m_ρ H(z_ρ)^2 at each centered zero. For Re z_ρ>0 this residue is nonzero. Other zeros cannot cancel a residue at a different complex location. If |F(t)|=O(exp(ηt)) for η≥0 then no pole in Re s>η, by identity theorem. Conversely if b≤η, the absolute bound holds. In particular RH is equivalent to boundedness of ONE fixed explicit F(t) on all t≥0. Quantitative corollary: limsup_{t→∞} log(1+|F(t)|)/t=b; for b>0 proof is lower bound by contradiction with analytic extension, for b=0 nonnegativity suffices. No spectral truncation or exchange of limits outside justified absolute region.

## C. One-sided version, and direct arithmetic bridge to (M)
Stronger explicit one-probe criterion: for η≥0, the upper bound F(t)≤Cη exp(ηt) for all sufficiently large t, without a lower bound, already implies b≤η. Proof: enlarge Cη so g(t)=Cη exp(ηt)−F(t)≥0 for all t≥0. Its Laplace transform G(s)=Cη/(s−η)−ΣH(zρ)^2/(s−zρ) for Re s>max(η,1/2). Suppose an off-line zero with Re zρ>η. Its pole remains (nonreal; no nontrivial real zeta zeros in (0,1), e.g. ζ(s)=η_D(s)/(1−2^(1−s))<0 by alternating positive eta sum). Let σc be the real abscissa of convergence of the nonnegative function g. The pole forces σc≥Re zρ>η: otherwise the Laplace transform would be holomorphic on Re s>σc, contradicting meromorphic continuation. It is finite by the exponential upper bound on F. Since σc>η and no centered zero is positive real, the meromorphic expression is analytic at σc. Contradiction to Landau's theorem for nonnegative Laplace transforms: choose a real σ0>σc sufficiently close that the analytic disk around σc extends a Taylor series centered at σ0 to a real σ1<σc. Taylor coefficients in the leftward variable are nonnegative moments ∫t^n e^(−σ0 t)g(t)dt/n!. By monotone convergence, their convergent Taylor sum equals ∫e^(−σ1 t)g(t)dt, impossible. This justifies no absolute-bound assumption and no hidden cancellation of nonreal poles. Reverse follows from B. Same argument applies to −F and hence either sign of one-sided bound. This is a classical-type Landau argument, not the published theta-lift inequality, and not a new proof of RH.

Direct connection to primes: k(v)=∫h(y+v)h(y)dy, supported [−2,2], even. Cross kernel for τ_t h vs h is k(u−t). For t>2, it vanishes near u=0. Danesh §5 gives additive explicit formula via multiplicative Mellin; retain the full prime-power term (the rendered aligned formula's second row is not shown by web text, but the preceding eq 34 and prose explicitly include it). Then
F(t)=exp(t/2) A − Σ_{n≥2} Λ(n)n^(−1/2) k(log n−t) + E(t),
A=∫k(v)e^(v/2)dv=H(1/2)²>0,
E(t)=−∫ k(u−t)e^(−5u/2)/(1−e^(−2u))du = O(exp(−5t/2)) for t≥3.
Algebra: e^(−u/2)−e^(−u/2)/(1−e^(−2u)) = −e^(−5u/2)/(1−e^(−2u)). Let x=e^t, W(x)=Σ Λ(n)(x/n)^(1/2) k(log(n/x)). Then sqrt(x)F(log x)=x A−W(x)+O(x^(−2)). RH is equivalent to the single fixed-shape inequality x A−W(x)≤C sqrt(x) for ALL sufficiently large x (or reverse sign); more generally a bound O(x^(1/2+η)) on either side excludes zeros with β>1/2+η. The sum is over [e^(−2)x,e²x]. No claim that our earlier local numerical bounds control this. The difficulty is prime-power cancellation with absolute square-root accuracy uniformly as x→∞. This gives a bridge between old Weil project and new Alpöge one-sided prime estimate. Arbitrarily large isolated samples of x do not suffice (see phase test); cofinal entire windows would require extra sparse-window Landau reasoning, not asserted here. Also no claim of an unconditional estimate for W(x).

## D. Independent local bounds / shrink-rate audit
Danesh Lemma 1 lower estimate can be strengthened by a simple bathtub bound: for support length d<log 2, normalize ||f||₂=1 and write Fourier density p(y)≤d/(2π), ∫p=1. Since log(e+|y|) increases radially, integral is minimized under just this cap by p=d/(2π) on [−π/d,π/d]. Proof subtract threshold value at π/d and compare the sign pointwise against this indicator. Thus integral ≥ (d/π)∫₀^{π/d}log(e+y)dy ≥log(π/d)−1 (use log(e+y)≥log y and integrate). With Ω≥log(e+|y|)−C0 and pole lower −2de^(d/2), obtain Q(f)≥[log(π/d)−1−C0−2de^(d/2)]||f||². Improves leading coefficient from 1/2 to 1 in the paper's elementary short-support lower bound; standard rearrangement, novelty not claimed. No prime shifts for this support length. Still no global positivity because cross terms remain.
Also Danesh Lemma 2's partition cost m log m with m≈1/w(t+const) gives explicit weaker criterion when w(L)=exp(−cL), c>0: uniform bound R≤exp(ηL) implies F_h(t)=O(exp((η+c+ε)t)) for every ε>0; then no zeta zero with β>1/2+η+c. For reciprocal or subexponential width c=0 recovers original exponent. This is only a conditional implication; cannot replace c by 0 for exponential widths using that estimate. Not claimed optimal.
