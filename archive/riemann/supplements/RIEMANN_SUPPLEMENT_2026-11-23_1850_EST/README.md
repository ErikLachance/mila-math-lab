## New independent exact computational tests (all local, not RH proofs)

(1) **Optimal sqrt balance and the source exponents.** `src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/PageFix.lean`, `PageWin`, gives for each valid sample: `n` between x^(2+8eps) and twice that; `m` between sqrt(x δ1) and twice; `k` between x^(9/2+10eps) and twice. `src/Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/DataParams₂.lean` defines δ1=x^(−eps/8). Therefore, for x≥1 eps>0, m∈[x^(1/2−eps/16),2x^(1/2−eps/16)]. Independently, the guide's Part II estimate (A) has error `x/m+x*m/k + m + k/n^2`. Exact algebra yields four exponent budgets (upper constants 1,2,2,2 respectively):
  - `x/m ≤ x^(1/2+eps/16)`;
  - `x*m/k ≤ 2x^(−3−161eps/16)`;
  - `m ≤ 2x^(1/2−eps/16)`;
  - `k/n² ≤ 2x^(1/2−6eps)`.
Hence sum ≤`7x^(1/2+eps/16)` for x≥1. This conditional bridge shows precisely the sqrt cancellation from m lift steps and why an extra x^(Ceps) loss still permits exponent 1/2+O(eps). It DOES NOT prove the main inequality (M), uniform existence of the samples, or H12. Script `power_budget_audit.py` checks the four fractional exponents and their domination exactly for eps 1/1000, 1/200, 1/52, 1/32, 1/16; five cases pass. Script `theta_balance_audit.py` checks 27 exact rational/integer samples x=t^(2q) (q=32,64,128), and m at 1,3/2,2 times target for t=2,3,5. All pass. These are sanity checks, not complete formalizations; the algebraic inequality is proved by the displayed exponents.

(2) **Source example check AND a minor prose error found.** The guide Part II §4.1 states row indicator `v(r)=(1+χ_q(r))/2`, correctly, but immediately after it incorrectly says row **1 is for nonsquares**. Quadratic nonsquares have χ_q=-1, hence **row 0**; row 1 corresponds to quadratic residues χ_q=+1. We independently recomputed using exact modular arithmetic with its example q=11, d=1319, N=16, primes≤61 in script `residue_table_audit.py`. All eight cells MATCH the paper's numerical table exactly: row0 (col1:[17], col3:[13,29,61], col5:[], col7:[41]); row1 (col1:[47], col3:[], col5:[37,59], col7:[23]). So the inconsistent sentence is a **harmless descriptive typo in an unofficial human guide, NOT a flaw in the Lean proof**. This is a real small falsification finding, not a sensational objection.

(3) **Analytic pole cancellation at s=1**. Rubinstein Part II Eq (II.1) writes `F(s)=−ζ'(s)/(s(s−1)ζ(s)) − 1/(s−1)^2 + (1+γ)/(s−1)` for Re(s)>1 as Mellin transform of normalized prime sum G. Script `mellin_pole_audit.py` uses formal Laurent expansion `ζ(1+t)=1/t+γ+c1*t+c2*t²+...` and SymPy algebra: coefficient of t^−2 is 0, coefficient t^−1 is 0; finite part `−2*c1+γ²+γ+1`. This independently confirms the needed cancellation of artificial s=1 pole. It does NOT show RH, convergence outside Re(s)>1, or absence of poles at zeros.

(4) **Landau sparse-interval lemma numerical constants check (rigorous rational inequalities)**. Rubinstein Part II §1 states η=max(0.8e^.2,1.2e^−.2), c=.008=1/125, σ≥252, with two inequalities required by gamma-kernel localization. `landau_constants_audit.py` proves via exact Fraction Taylor exponential upper and lower bounds (not float): right η branch dominates (`e^.4<1.5`); `c<log((1+η)/(2η))` by the equivalent `(6/5)*(2e^c−1)<e^.2`; and `(σ−θ)/(σ−2)<e^c` for all σ≥252 and 0<θ<1 since maximum ratio at σ=252, θ=0 is 126/125<e^c. Four exact fraction checks pass. Only constants are certified here; the Mellin pole / nearest-pole Cauchy argument and full lemma are not independently reproved.
